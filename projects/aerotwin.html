Purpose & Necessity

AeroTwin is a bi-directional 3D digital twin of Indian airport ground and terminal-area operations, built around a real, surveyed model of Vadodara Airport (VABO) and a generic airport builder that extends the same simulation core to Delhi, Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Pune, and Surat. "Bi-directional" is the operative word — the browser isn't a static viewer. Posting a disruption (a runway closure, fog, a taxiway shutdown) mutates the live SimPy simulation on the server, and every connected client sees the consequence within a frame: aircraft holding short, arrivals going around, ground traffic re-routing around the closure.

Key Technical Implementation

Ground and runway conflicts are prevented by construction, not by tuning: every piece of pavement an aircraft could occupy is a single-occupancy resource, and a taxi clearance is only granted once every resource along the route is acquired in one canonical order — making deadlock and two-aircraft-in-the-same- place structurally impossible. A Dijkstra router plans taxi clearances the way a real ground controller would, preferring taxiways to taxilanes and rapid exits to right-angle turn-offs, while the runway itself is modeled as a priority resource with wake-turbulence separation and crosswind/visibility minima. Each airport's pavement, markings, and buildings are generated server-side from a compact structural schema (runway thresholds, a taxi graph, stand positions), so the rendered picture can never drift from what the simulation believes is true. An ML model registry layers on taxi-time regression, delay forecasting, congestion-tier clustering, and weather ground-stop probability — falling back to a documented heuristic when no trained model is present, so the twin still runs standalone. Safety isn't just asserted: a headless soak-test script runs every airport under both nominal traffic and a deliberately hostile disruption sequence, auditing actual inter-aircraft distances every frame against the 55m ground / 300m-120ft airborne separation standards.
